# Retraining the f7ip models on the relabelled 20 Feb file (5 Nov 2026)

All 42 `f7ip` models (XGBoost, Random Forest, linear SVM; all-tools seeds 0–4; HOIC / LOIC-HTTP / LOIC-UDP held
out with the per-day, by-file and attack-period benign splits) were retrained with
`--files Tuesday-20-02-2018_relabelled.csv Wednesday-21-02-2018_victim_capture_labelled.csv`
(12 training runs, 44 minutes). The only change in the data is `scripts\pcap\relabel_feb20.py`
(see `scripts\pcap\README.md`, item 9): 1,629 of the 7,948,748 rows of the published 20 Feb file get a different
label, and its 12-hour timestamps become 24-hour. The app's test suite passes with the new models (139 passed).

## What changed in the data

| Rows | Published label | Corrected label | What they are |
|---|---|---|---|
| 797 | DDoS attacks-LOIC-HTTP | LOIC-UDP | The 20 Feb LOIC-UDP flood (13:14–13:29): attacker UDP to the victim's port 80, 32-byte payloads, ~134,000 packets per flow, no replies |
| 702 | Benign | LOIC-UDP | The victim's ICMP port-unreachable replies to that flood, recorded as UDP flows by CICFlowMeter |
| 130 | Benign | LOIC-HTTP | LOIC-HTTP connections recorded server-to-attacker by CICFlowMeter's timeout quirk |

LOIC-UDP rows across both days: 3,328 → 4,827 (flood 2,527, ICMP replies 2,300). The LOIC-UDP test sets below
therefore have 4,827 rows instead of 3,328, and the all-tools 30% test split has 1,448 instead of 999.

## The three things the retrain shows

1. **The earlier "LOIC-UDP held out = 0.52" was not a held-out result.** With the published labels, the 797 flood
   flows of 20 Feb were in the *training* set under the LOIC-HTTP label, so a model that had "never seen LOIC-UDP"
   had in fact seen 797 of its flood flows. With the labels corrected, every LOIC-UDP-held-out model (all three
   model types, all three benign splits, XGBoost seeds 0, 1 and 2) catches **0 of the 2,527 flood flows at every
   threshold down to 0.01** (XGBoost PR-AUC 0.004). The six f7ip groups do not carry over to an unseen UDP flood:
   its window features are tiny (each flow lasts the full 120 s), and "no reply" alone is not an attack signal in
   this data (the 20 Feb benign set has many unanswered flows). Say this in the report in place of the 0.52
   "ceiling" wording in `feature_search_findings_2026-11-05.md`, item 6 of its Limits, and in the update-5 claim
   that the set carried over to an unseen tool for all three models (it does for HOIC and LOIC-HTTP, not LOIC-UDP).
2. **LOIC-HTTP held out (per-day benign split) is threshold-sensitive, not seed-sensitive.** XGBoost: recall 0.010
   at threshold 0.5 (was 0.960), 0.48 at 0.2–0.05, **0.96 at 0.01** with FPR 0.0005; seeds 1 and 2 give the same
   curve (0.008 / 0.48 / 0.96); PR-AUC 0.9986. So the model still ranks LOIC-HTTP flows above benign, but their
   scores now sit between 0.01 and 0.5. The by-file split moved the other way (0.011 → 0.481 at 0.5; 0.96 at
   0.01) and the attack-period split is unchanged (0.963 → 0.958). The per-day split itself changed with the
   timestamp fix: with the published 12-hour stamps, the afternoon rows ("01:00–07:59") sorted *before* the
   morning, so "the earlier 70% of the benign flows" was a different set of rows. Random Forest and the SVM barely
   moved (RF 0.499 → 0.483, SVM 0.978 → 0.971). Treat the XGBoost LOIC-HTTP-held-out number as "0.48–0.96
   depending on the threshold" and show the threshold sweep rather than one number.
3. **Everything else is the same or slightly better.** All-tools XGBoost: HOIC 1.000, LOIC-HTTP 1.000, LOIC-UDP
   0.986–0.990 across seeds (was 0.970–0.989), false alarms 5–10 (was 25–34). RF LOIC-UDP 0.970–0.975 (was
   0.944–0.965). SVM unchanged at ~0.50 on LOIC-UDP (it never flags the ICMP reply rows). HOIC held out: XGBoost
   1.000 on every split; RF by-file 0.762 → 0.999, per-day 0.688 → 0.755.
